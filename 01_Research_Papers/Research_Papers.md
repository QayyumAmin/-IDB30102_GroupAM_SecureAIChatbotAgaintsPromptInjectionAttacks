# Research papers (Assignment 1, entries 1–10)

| 1 | 2024 | Nicholas Carlini, Matthew Jagielski, Christopher A. Choquette-Choo, Daniel Paleka, Will Pearce, Hyrum Anderson, Andreas Terzis, Kurt Thomas, Florian Tramèr | Show that poisoning web-scale AI training datasets is practically feasible and expose weaknesses in current data-collection pipelines. | Web-scraped and crowdsourced datasets, including LAION-400M and COYO-700M; experiments covered 10 popular datasets and mutable web/Wikipedia sources. | Two attacks: split-view poisoning changes content after validation but before download; frontrunning poisoning injects malicious content shortly before periodic dataset snapshots. Focuses on data provenance and collection security. | Poisonable fraction of dataset, attack cost, number of datasets affected, and timing required for poisoning. | Both attacks were practical at web scale. About 0.01% of LAION-400M or COYO-700M could be poisoned for roughly US$60, and 10 popular datasets were shown to be vulnerable. | Success depends on mutable data sources and collection timing. Scalable provenance, immutable snapshots, hashing/integrity checks, and secure dataset ingestion remain important research needs. |
| 2 | 2024 | Siyuan Liang, Mingli Zhu, Aishan Liu, Baoyuan Wu, Xiaochun Cao, Ee-Chien Chang | Develop a stealthy backdoor attack against CLIP-style multimodal contrastive learning that remains effective after backdoor detection and clean fine-tuning. | About 500K CC3M image-text pairs for poisoning; ImageNet-1K for downstream zero-shot/linear-probe evaluation; pre-trained OpenAI CLIP. | BadCLIP uses dual-embedding guidance: textual-embedding consistency plus visual-embedding resistance to optimize a natural-looking trigger aligned with the target semantics. Tested under fine-tuning and CleanCLIP defense. | Clean Accuracy (CA), Attack Success Rate (ASR), and backdoor detector P_L1 norm. | Achieved about 58.60% clean accuracy and 98.81% ASR without defense. ASR remained about 92.50% after ordinary fine-tuning and 89.60% after CleanCLIP, showing strong persistence. | Evaluation mainly targets CLIP/multimodal contrastive models and selected downstream tasks. Stronger MCL-specific detection and mitigation, plus broader real-world/physical testing, are needed. |
| 3 | 2024 | Yuancheng Xu, Jiarui Yao, Manli Shu, Yanchao Sun, Zichu Wu, Ning Yu, Tom Goldstein, Furong Huang | Demonstrate a stealthy data-poisoning attack that manipulates vision-language model responses to ordinary image prompts while preserving normal model utility. | cc-sbu-align with 3,500 image-description pairs; 200 images per original/destination concept and 200 test images per original concept. LLaVA-1.5 and MiniGPT-v2 were evaluated. | Shadowcast perturbs destination-concept images so their visual features mimic an original concept, while paired text keeps the destination semantics. Uses PGD; includes label and persuasion attacks, with GPT-3.5-assisted caption/evaluation steps. | Attack Success Rate (ASR), GQA and VizWiz scores, plus human ratings of coherence/relevance. | Fewer than 1% poisoned samples (30 examples) produced over 60% ASR. At >1.4% poisoning, ASR exceeded 95% for Trump-to-Biden and 80% for EngineLight-to-FuelLight while benchmark utility stayed close to clean performance. | The study does not develop a defense. Effective filtering/adversarial training for VLMs is still difficult because of multimodal architectures, computational cost, and possible utility loss. |
| 4 | 2024 | Patrick Chao, Edoardo Debenedetti, Alexander Robey, Maksym Andriushchenko, Francesco Croce, Vikash Sehwag, Edgar Dobriban, Nicolas Flammarion, George J. Pappas, Florian Tramèr, Hamed Hassani, Eric Wong | Create a standardized, reproducible benchmark for evaluating LLM jailbreak attacks and defenses because previous studies used inconsistent behaviors, metrics, and evaluation procedures. | JBB-Behaviors: 100 harmful behaviors with 100 matched benign behaviors; evaluated on Vicuna-13B-v1.5, Llama-2-7B-chat, GPT-3.5-Turbo and GPT-4. | JailbreakBench provides a common attack/defense pipeline, benchmark artifacts, several jailbreak attacks (including GCG, PAIR and Prompt+Random Search), baseline defenses, and a Llama-3-70B safety judge. | Attack Success Rate (ASR), average query/token cost, and judge agreement/error rates. | Prompt+Random Search reached about 89% ASR on Vicuna, 90% on Llama-2, 93% on GPT-3.5 and 78% on GPT-4. The automated judge achieved roughly 90.7% agreement with human labels. | The benchmark restricts some attacker capabilities and proprietary model behavior can change after safety updates. Broader adaptive attacks, system-prompt threats, and continuously updated defenses are needed. |
| 5 | 2024 | Yupei Liu, Yuqi Jia, Runpeng Geng, Jinyuan Jia, Neil Zhenqiang Gong | Formalize prompt-injection attacks on LLM-integrated applications and systematically compare attacks and defenses instead of relying on isolated case studies. | Seven NLP tasks covering duplicate detection, grammar correction, hate detection, NLI, sentiment, spam detection and summarization; 10 LLMs were evaluated. | A general prompt-injection framework, a new Combined Attack built from existing attack techniques, and a benchmark of 5 attacks against 10 prevention/detection defenses using the Open-Prompt-Injection platform. | PNA, Attack Success Value (ASV), Matching Rate (MR), and false-positive/false-negative rates for detection defenses. | The Combined Attack consistently outperformed prior attacks, while none of the 10 defenses fully solved prompt injection. Some defenses also reduced normal task utility; paraphrasing caused an average target-task utility drop of about 0.14. | Attack construction is still largely heuristic and existing defenses trade security for utility. More optimization-based attacks and integrated prevention, detection, and recovery mechanisms are required. |
| 6 | 2024 | Jiahao Yu, Xingwei Lin, Zheng Yu, Xinyu Xing | Automate and scale assessment of jailbreak vulnerabilities in large language models because manual jailbreak discovery is slow and difficult to reproduce. | 77 human-written jailbreak seed templates and 100 unethical questions; 7,700 ChatGPT responses were manually labeled to train/evaluate the jailbreak oracle. | LLM-Fuzzer applies fuzz testing with seed-selection strategies and LLM-based mutation operators to generate new jailbreak prompts. A RoBERTa-large oracle classifies whether generated responses are successful jailbreaks. | JQN, ASR, EASR, query budget/cost measures, and oracle classification accuracy. | The oracle achieved 96.16% accuracy. On GPT-3.5 the fuzzer reached about 96.85/100 jailbroken questions, 89.20% ASR and 93.14% EASR; generated prompts also transferred to multiple other LLMs. | The method depends heavily on the accuracy of the response oracle; false positives/negatives can misguide fuzzing. Better jailbreak judges and evaluation on newer, strongly red-teamed models are needed. |
| 7 | 2024 | Shaokui Wei, Hongyuan Zha, Baoyuan Wu | Defend neural networks against training-time backdoor attacks without relying only on detecting, removing, or relabeling suspicious poisoned samples. | CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet and GTSRB; evaluated with PreAct-ResNet18, VGG19-BN and ViT-B/16 against seven representative backdoor attacks. | PDB (Proactive Defensive Backdoor) intentionally injects a secret defender-controlled trigger and uses a reversible label mapping. At inference, the defensive trigger is applied and the predicted label is reversed to suppress the malicious backdoor. | Benign Accuracy (ACC), Attack Success Rate (ASR), Defense Effectiveness Rating (DER), and runtime. | On CIFAR-10 with PreAct-ResNet18 at 5% poisoning, PDB averaged about 91.36% ACC, only 0.71% ASR and 96.39 DER, substantially suppressing several backdoor attacks with limited clean-accuracy loss. | PDB assumes the defender controls training and has reserved benign data. Its robustness should be validated across more tasks, architectures, adaptive attackers, and practical deployment settings. |
| 8 | 2024 | Sunay Bhat, Jeffrey Jiang, Omead Pooladzandi, Alexander Branch, Gregory Pottie | Design a universal train-time poisoning defense that can remove diverse poisoned examples while preserving classifier generalization and avoiding attack-specific assumptions. | CIFAR-10, CINIC-10 and Tiny-ImageNet; evaluated against attacks such as Narcissus, Gradient Matching and Bullseye Polytope, with external datasets used to train generative purifiers. | PureGen preprocesses training samples using stochastic purification based on Energy-Based Models (EBMs), Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs), or a hybrid of both before classifier training. | Poison success rate, natural/clean accuracy, maximum poison success, FID/distribution shift, and purification/training time. | For CIFAR-10 Gradient Matching, poison success dropped from 44% without defense to 0% with PureGen-DDPM and about 1% with PureGen-EBM, while natural accuracy remained around 90.93% and 92.98%, respectively. | Generative purification adds substantial computation and can reduce clean accuracy. DDPM-based purification is particularly slow; efficient scaling and stronger evaluation on broader attacks/data modalities remain open. |
| 9 | 2023 | Thuy Dung Nguyen, Tuan A Nguyen, Anh Tran, Khoa D Doan, Kok-Seng Wong | Create a stealthy and durable backdoor attack in federated learning that remains active even after malicious clients stop participating. | MNIST, CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet under heterogeneous/non-IID federated partitions; typically 10 of 200 clients participated per training round. | IBA (Irreversible Backdoor Attack) learns an instance-specific, visually stealthy trigger and gradually injects the backdoor into selected model parameters. Tested with FedAvg/FedProx/FedNova and multiple FL defenses. | Main-task Accuracy (MA), Backdoor Accuracy (BA), durability/decay after attacker removal, and stealth/anomaly measures. | Across datasets and aggregators, IBA achieved about 87.13%-99.77% BA with little main-task degradation. On CIFAR-10, it retained about 83.16% BA after 250 additional clean rounds, showing strong persistence. | Some robust aggregation defenses still reduce attack effectiveness, and evaluation covers a limited set of FL conditions. Stronger defenses and broader non-IID, client-scale, and trigger settings remain needed. |
| 10 | 2023 | Wenxiao Wang, Soheil Feizi | Develop a more meaningful robustness model for data poisoning when an attacker can inject very large numbers of samples, making a simple poison-count budget unrealistic. | HuffPost News Category Dataset: 191,939 headlines in 41 categories across 71 months; temporal experiments use continuously collected data and later months for testing. | Defines temporal attack budgets using earliness and duration, introduces temporal robustness, builds a continuous collection/periodic deployment benchmark, and proposes temporal aggregation with provable guarantees. Uses RoBERTa features with a linear classifier. | Robust fraction under bounded earliness/duration, clean accuracy, and inference overhead. | Temporal aggregation provided meaningful poisoning protection even when the number of poisoned samples was unbounded, provided the attack was temporally bounded. Larger aggregation windows improved robustness with a modest performance/latency trade-off. | The threat model relies on partially trustworthy timestamps and was evaluated mainly on a news-text stream. More modalities, real production pipelines, combined time/count budgets, and lower-overhead defenses should be studied. |
